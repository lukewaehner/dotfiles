# Stockholder litigation and appraisal

Load when: the target is a public company and any of these holds: a controlling or large holder sits on
both sides (rollover, equity in the buyer, a post-closing role); approval came by written consent at or
soon after signing; a holder is selling under unusual liquidity pressure or receives a payment others do
not; the agreement preserves appraisal rights; the deal is a going-private (Rule 13e-3) transaction; or a
note or headline mentions class actions, fiduciary claims, "investigations" or dissenting holders. On a
cash take-private at least one of these is usually true.

## Why it matters for the spread

Stockholder suits rarely stop a cash deal with a real premium, but they can move the disclosure clock,
change the cash the buyer must fund at closing, and create a post-closing claim that draws event-driven
holders. Appraisal never blocks closing; it shifts cash out of the closing and into a later claim. The job
is to say what litigation can do to completion, timing and closing cash, from the documents and dockets.
Never give a probability that a suit is filed, survives or succeeds, and never advise either side. Label
opinions "Assessment:".

## Doctrine reference [verify every point before citing]

Confirm each case or statute with `caselaw-search` or a dated secondary source, and cite source and date.
This area changed in 2024 and 2025; write "unverified" next to anything not confirmed.

- Controller transactions: entire fairness (price and process) is the default unless both a special
  committee and a majority-of-the-minority vote were conditions from the start (Kahn v. M&F Worldwide,
  Del. 2014). In re Match Group (Del. 2024) kept that default for controller conflicts. [verify]
- Delaware SB 21 (March 2025) rewrote DGCL 144: defines control, and for going-private deals requires an
  independent committee and approval by a majority of disinterested votes cast to reach the safe harbor.
  Applies except to litigation pending or demands made on or before 2/17/2025. Check for later rulings on
  scope or constitutionality. [verify]
- Written consent: a controller's consent is not a disinterested vote. Whether it was informed turns on
  the information statement, which usually is not filed yet.
- Disclosure claims against an information statement or 13E-3 usually seek supplemental disclosure.
  Which federal provisions apply to an information statement versus a proxy is a counsel question; check
  what complaints on comparable deals invoke.
- Books-and-records (DGCL 220): the 2025 amendments narrowed what can be demanded [verify]. A 220 demand
  is often the first public sign of a complaint in preparation.
- Fair value in appraisal: in an arm's-length, well-run process the Delaware Supreme Court has leaned on
  deal price (Dell v. Magnetar, 2017; DFC Global v. Muirfield, 2017; Verition v. Aruba, 2019, deal price
  less synergies). A conflicted process gives that reasoning less support. [verify]

## Appraisal (DGCL 262) mechanics [verify against current text]

- Cash consideration defeats the market-out exception for listed stock, so appraisal is available in a
  cash merger.
- Merger by written consent: notice goes out before the effective date or within 10 days after; holders
  may demand within 20 days after the notice is given (262(d)(2)).
- De minimis exception for listed shares: the court dismisses unless the shares seeking appraisal exceed
  1% of the class or their consideration exceeds $1 million (262(g)). Compute the share count that equals
  $1 million at the deal price rather than assuming the exception bites; it usually does not.
- Interest: 5% over the Federal Reserve discount rate, compounded quarterly, from the effective date to
  payment (262(h)); the company can stop it by prepaying.
- Petition deadline: 120 days after the effective date. Beneficial owners as well as record holders may
  demand (since 2022).
- Effect on closing cash: dissenting shares are not paid at closing, so the closing cash need falls by
  the consideration on those shares and the buyer carries the claim. Size it as cash per 1% of the class
  at the deal price and add one line to sources and uses.
- Appraisal-out condition: if the agreement caps dissenting shares (commonly a percentage of the class),
  the buyer has a walk right once demands exceed it. Without that condition, appraisal demands give no
  walk right. Quote the clause or state that none exists.
- Incentives: statutory interest pays claimants while they wait; their risk is a finding below the deal
  price. State this as a fact about incentives, not a forecast of recoveries.

## What litigation can and cannot do

Write one line per channel.

1. Walk rights: is there a condition tied to litigation or appraisal (no injunction or illegality order,
   dissenting-share cap, no pending governmental action)? Check the MAE carve-out for deal litigation.
   A private stockholder suit is not a condition unless it produces an order.
2. Injunction: deal-blocking relief against a premium cash deal is rare. Check the docket for a motion
   to expedite, TRO or preliminary-injunction motion. Absent one, write "no injunction motion on the
   docket as of [date]". No number.
3. Timing: supplements and SEC comments can move mailing and the 20-day wait. Add a "disclosure-driven
   slippage" row to the clock build with days taken from the agreement's covenants. Ask counsel whether
   a supplement restarts the wait. Expedited discovery alone does not move a consent-based close.
4. Closing cash: appraisal lowers it; see above.
5. Settlement control: who controls the defense, whether buyer consent is needed to settle, cooperation
   duties.
6. Fees and indemnity: D&O indemnification and tail-insurance covenants show who bears the cost.
7. Post-closing claims: appraisal interest and any controller damages claim can attract event-driven
   holders; state it as an incentive fact.

## Live sweep (record date, tool and query each time)

- `docket-search` by party name for target and buyer, courts `delch` and `ded` plus the district where
  the target sits, filed after announcement; `docket-details` and `docket-filings` on any hit. Coverage
  comes from RECAP and is incomplete: an empty result means none found there, not a clean docket.
- `caselaw-search` (court `delch` or `del`) for controller-deal precedents, SB 21 follow-on rulings and
  recent appraisal decisions in controller cash deals; `caselaw-opinion` to read one.
- Web search for plaintiff-firm "investigation" releases and demand letters. These are advertisements,
  not filed suits; tag them [press].
- Target's latest 10-Q legal proceedings and subsequent-events notes; deal 8-K Items 8.01 and 5.07.
- Precedents with a controller or founder rollover via `arbjournal-precedents` or `semantic-search`
  (namespace `arbjournal`): did they draw suits, petitions or delay? Table: deal, controller or roll,
  approval method, suits or petitions, timing effect, source. If none found, list where you searched.
- Tag each finding by type and never blend: demand letter, class complaint, individual complaint, 220
  demand, appraisal petition, press-release investigation.

## How to conclude

`Assessment:` two to four sentences: which indicators are present, what litigation could do to the
closing date and closing cash, and what the 13E-3 or information statement will show that current
documents cannot (Background of the Merger, fairness opinion and projections, insider interests). End
with "What resolves it": the named filing and its expected date from the clock build.

When testing a broker note's litigation claim, name the mechanism it relies on (controller on both
sides, consent at signing, related-party payment, conflicted adviser, no minority vote), check whether
the documents show it, and cite the governing statute point. A claim with no mechanism is logged "no
mechanism given".

## Common errors

- Treating a law-firm "investigation" press release as a filed suit.
- Reporting an empty RECAP search as "no litigation".
- Assuming the de minimis exception defeats appraisal without computing the $1 million line.
- Saying appraisal threatens closing when the agreement has no dissenting-share condition.
- Citing doctrine from memory without a dated check, especially post-2025 Section 144 points.
- Inferring motive from a seller's pledge or sell-down instead of recording the fact.
- Putting a probability on suit, injunction or recovery.
- Letting this analysis displace the gating-regulator discussion; it is one row in the risk read.
