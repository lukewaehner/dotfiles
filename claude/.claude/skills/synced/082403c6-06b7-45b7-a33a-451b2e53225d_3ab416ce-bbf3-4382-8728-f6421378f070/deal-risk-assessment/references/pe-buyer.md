# Phases 2–3 Branch: Financial Sponsor Buyer

The sponsor fund competes with nobody directly — its antitrust exposure lives one layer down, in the portfolio, often across dozens of holdings, many private, some minority stakes, with no consolidated filing. The hard work here is *discovering* the competitive entity before screening it. Do not skip the enumeration and jump to "obvious" overlaps; the deal-relevant overlap is often a portfolio company nobody mentions in the announcement.

## Phase 2 — Sponsor and portfolio mapping

- **Enumerate the current portfolio — majority AND minority stakes.** Sources, in order of reliability: portfolio company EDGAR filings (13D/G, S-1s naming the sponsor, proxy ownership tables), the sponsor's own website portfolio page, 13F where applicable, reputable press and PE-database coverage found via web search. Mark each holding as verified (EDGAR/sponsor disclosure) or reported-only (press). Note stake type — minority stakes still matter to regulators (interlocks, information sharing, partial-ownership theories) and must be flagged, not dropped.
- **Historical portfolio and exits** in the target's sector: past platforms, add-on chains, exit outcomes. This establishes whether the deal fits a roll-up pattern.
- **Sponsor regulatory history**: prior FTC/DOJ/EC reviews, second requests, consent decrees, required divestitures, abandoned deals. Search agency press releases and trade press. A sponsor with a consent decree in the sector faces a different review than a first-timer.
- **Investment thesis and platform/add-on characterization**: is the target a new platform or an add-on to an existing one? Add-ons to an existing platform in the same market are the highest-scrutiny configuration.

**Deliverable — Acquirer Competitive Footprint table**: portfolio company | stake (majority/minority) | sector / product lines | verified via (cite) | relevance to target.

## Phase 3 — Overlap screen

Cross every footprint entry against the target's product/service lines and geographies:

- Classify each: direct horizontal / vertical (upstream or downstream — specify direction) / adjacent-complementary / potential future competition / none.
- For private portfolio companies without filings, use sponsor descriptions and press, cite them, and mark the classification as lower-confidence.
- Assess **roll-up seriality**: count sponsor acquisitions in the sector over the past 5–10 years. Regulators increasingly treat serial small acquisitions as cumulative concentration even when each cleared thresholds individually — a long add-on chain is itself a risk factor regardless of this deal's standalone overlap.

**Deliverable — Overlap Screen table**: portfolio company | overlap type | overlapping product/service line | overlap geography | evidence citation.

## Sponsor-specific theories of harm for Phase 4

Carry these into the deep competitive-effects work in addition to the standard theories:

- **Roll-up seriality**: the acquisition pattern as cumulative concentration.
- **Cross-portfolio bundling / cross-selling**: portfolio companies packaging offerings or steering customers.
- **Data advantage**: combined datasets across portfolio companies and target.
- **Common-ownership / interlock concerns** from minority stakes in competitors.
- **Vertical alignment across the portfolio**: portfolio companies as suppliers to or customers of the target creating foreclosure incentives that no single company's filings reveal.
