# Phases 2–3 Branch: Strategic Buyer

The acquirer IS the competitive entity, and it files. No discovery exercise is needed — the acquirer's own 10-K/10-Q substitutes for the portfolio mapping. The work here is a rigorous two-company comparison, and the risk profile skews toward the classic direct-horizontal case, which is often HIGHER-scrutiny than a PE deal: two named competitors merging is exactly what the agencies are built to catch.

## Phase 2 — Acquirer competitive footprint

From the acquirer's latest 10-K/10-Q:

- **Segments and business lines**: reproduce the segment structure with revenue by segment, cited.
- **Verbatim competitor list** from the acquirer's Competition section, cited. Check immediately whether the target appears in the acquirer's list or vice versa — a named-competitor merger is the single strongest overlap signal and should be flagged prominently.
- **Geographic revenue breakdown** from the acquirer's segment tables, verbatim with citation.
- **M&A history**: the acquirer's acquisitions over the past 5–10 years in the sector — this substitutes for sponsor roll-up analysis. Serial acquisitions of nascent competitors invite killer-acquisition scrutiny.
- **Acquirer regulatory history**: prior antitrust reviews, second requests, consent decrees, blocked or abandoned deals, existing behavioral remedies still in force. An acquirer operating under a consent decree faces a different review.
- **Strategic rationale** as stated in the announcement press release, investor deck, and proxy — synergy claims about pricing, capacity, or customer consolidation are read by regulators as admissions. If the deck maps the parties' own **pro-forma combined share / rank / "#1 in X"** position, capture it verbatim with the slide cite — a company's self-drawn combined-market map is among the strongest overlap evidence available.

**Deliverable — Acquirer Competitive Footprint table**: segment / business line | revenue (cite) | disclosed competitors in that line (verbatim, cite) | relevance to target.

## Phase 3 — Overlap screen

Cross each acquirer segment/line against the target's product/service lines and geographies:

- Classify each: direct horizontal / vertical (specify direction) / adjacent-complementary / potential future competition / none.
- **Mutual-listing check**: state explicitly whether each party names the other as a competitor in filings, with citations. If yes, the horizontal case is largely made by the parties' own disclosures.
- Vertical check: is either party a disclosed supplier or customer of the other (customer-concentration disclosures, supply-agreement 8-Ks)?

**Deliverable — Overlap Screen table**: acquirer segment/line | overlap type | overlapping target line | overlap geography | evidence citation.

## Strategic-specific theories of harm for Phase 4

Carry these into the deep competitive-effects work in addition to the standard theories:

- **Direct horizontal unilateral effects**: head-to-head competition eliminated; closeness-of-competition evidence from mutual competitor listings, overlapping customers, win/loss dynamics in trade press.
- **Coordinated effects**: post-merger market structure conducive to tacit coordination among remaining rivals.
- **Killer acquisition / entrenchment**: acquiring a nascent or potential competitor to neutralize it — assess the target's pipeline/R&D versus the acquirer's incumbency.
- **Vertical foreclosure with existing relationships**: if pre-deal supply/customer links exist, foreclosure incentives are concrete, not hypothetical.
- **Synergy-claim exposure**: cost synergies framed around capacity rationalization or pricing are regulatory ammunition; note them.

## Phase 5 addendum — acquirer balance-sheet capacity

Unlike an LBO with committed financing, strategic deals may rely on the acquirer's balance sheet or stock. Assess from the acquirer's filings: cash and revolver capacity vs. cash consideration; bridge/permanent financing disclosures; rating-agency actions if reported; for stock consideration, the exchange ratio and any collar. A strained acquirer adds financing risk even absent a financing condition.
