# State health care approvals — perimeter test, clocks, and output template

**Why this file exists.** On the OPCH / CD&R-McKesson deal (October 2026) the first review named the gate
"state pharmacy licensing", borrowed from a different deal, and recorded the real list of state approvals as
"unfiled" because it sits in a confidential Disclosure Letter. Three broker notes then did the work the review
had skipped: one tested state statutes against OPCH's footprint, one ranked states by site count, one gave a
single date with no state detail. Two lessons carry into every deal with a multi-state health care target:

1. The state list is almost never public on day one (the target's own earlier merger proxy said only "certain
   states"). "List unfiled" is a sourcing gap to log, **never the end of the question.** Derive the list.
2. State health care transaction laws are **threshold-triggered**, not licence-triggered. A 10-K "Regulation"
   section lists licences; it does not list which revenue, asset or location thresholds a new owner trips. That
   is why the licensing-style perimeter in Agent E missed them.

**Status of the facts below.** Laws in this area change every year. Every row carries a source tag:
**[agency]** read on the agency's own page on 10/6/2026, **[Ropes]** or **[Foley]** secondary law-firm sources
read the same day, **[general]** general knowledge not yet checked against a primary source. Agent E re-checks
every row it relies on, live, and writes the check date next to it. A row you did not re-check is labelled
"carried from v13, not re-verified".

---

## The three layers

| Layer | What it is | Trigger | Usually on the critical path? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Transaction notice / review laws** | State laws that require advance notice, and sometimes approval or a cost-and-market review, before a health care deal closes | Revenue, asset or in-state presence thresholds, measured at the party or parent level | Can be: long lead times (CA, ME) and comprehensive reviews (OR, MA, CA) set the earliest and latest dates |
| **B. Licensure and change of ownership** | Pharmacy (resident and non-resident), home infusion, home health and similar licences; certificate of need in the states that still have it | The licence held in that state | Often, because the slowest single state sets the date (precedent: Walgreens/Sycamore closed 108 days after HSR expiry on state work) **[general]**, so name the slow states |
| **C. Federal program and accreditation** | Medicare and Medicaid enrollment ownership changes, DEA registrations, accreditation notices | Participation in the program | Rarely; usually notification. Confirm, say so, and move on |

Layer A has a statute you can read. Layer B needs a state-by-state licence list from the target's own disclosure
or licensing pages, tiered fast / moderate / slow from precedent. Layer C is a one-line check.

---

## Perimeter test — how to decide whether a state is in

1. **Test the statute's own measure, not a proxy.** Revenue and asset thresholds are the test. **Site counts are
   a proxy** and must be labelled as one. A state with one site can still be in (revenue test met) and a state
   with ten sites can be out (no transaction law). A note that says "OR (1) and MA (3) are smaller footprints,
   unclear if required" has substituted a proxy for the test.
2. **Measure at the right level.** Massachusetts counts the revenue of the "uppermost corporate parent in the
   business of health care and all affiliates", including revenue outside Massachusetts **[agency]**. For a
   strategic buyer such as McKesson, the parent-level test is easily met. Maine's new law is aimed at private
   equity and similar buyers acquiring majority control **[Foley]**, so the sponsor's role decides coverage.
   Always test: the target, the acquirer's ultimate parent, and any sponsor or its affiliates the statute counts.
3. **Separate three questions.** (a) Does the statute apply to the parties? (b) Is it notice-only, review, or prior
   approval? (c) Is the filing a listed closing condition in the agreement? A statute can require a filing even
   when the agreement's confidential list omits it. The agreement's list decides whether the parties may close
   without the consent; the statute decides whether the filing is legally required. State all three.
4. **Use the target's own history.** A state notice the target filed in an earlier deal is a primary record that
   the state treated it as covered. Agencies post these (Oregon posted OPCH's 2023 Amedisys notice **[agency]**).
   Also read the target's and acquirer's earlier merger proxies for a "State Regulatory Approvals" paragraph, and
   say if it only says "certain states".
5. **Pull the agencies' public notice pages** for the new deal. A filing that appears there is a monitorable and
   belongs on the watch list even before any proxy.

California thresholds **[agency]**: annual revenue or California assets of at least $25M; or at least $10M while
transacting with an entity that meets the $25M test; or a party that provides services in a designated primary
care shortage area. Oregon thresholds were given as $25M for one party and $10M for another, on a three-year
average, by one broker note; **not verified against the administrative rule**.

**Notice-only versus approval matters for the critical path.** Only prior-approval regimes (Maine, New Mexico,
Oregon's review) can stop a closing outright, and only long lead-time notices (California 90 days, Maine 180 days)
can set a hard earliest date. A notice-only regime (New York) belongs in the table as a line, not as a gate.
Classify every row: **notice-only / review with possible referral / prior approval**.

## Floors and tails — the arithmetic

For each state in scope compute two dates from the **filing date**, not from announcement. Use
`scripts/deal_clock.py adddays --start <filing date> --n <days> --base <base close> --outside <outside date>`.

- **Lead-time floor** = filing date + the notice lead time. The deal cannot close before this date.
- **Tail** = filing date + the comprehensive-review outer bound. Show it against the base close and the outside
  date. A tail that ends well inside the outside date is a timing risk, not a completion risk. Say which it is.

Compute each at two filing dates: the agreement's **latest permitted date** (health care filings are often due
30 business days after signing; count with `deal_clock.py bdays` on the agreement's own calendar) and an
**early-filing** date. Clocks that restart on a deficiency or an information request (Oregon explicitly) are
shown as "from complete notice, before any tolling", and the table says so.

**A conditional lead-time rule must be run down, not ignored.** Where a state regime is possible and its lead time
would move the earliest close past the base case (Maine in the OPCH example), write the arithmetic under its
condition, label it conditional, name the fact that decides it (does the law cover this buyer and this target,
and how does it treat pending deals), and put that fact in the watch list.

## Precedent rows — era and basis

- **Count from filing as well as from announcement.** Brokers' comparables count from announcement and assume
  an immediate filing. Where the agreement allows up to 30 business days to file, that assumption can hide six
  weeks.
- **Tag every state-regime precedent with the regime era.** A deal announced before a state's law took effect
  did not face that review. For each Layer A state in scope, Agent E fetches the effective date and tags each
  precedent "before [state] regime" or "after". A comp set that predates the regimes now in scope is a floor on
  duration, not a central estimate. (For Maine, the review process starts 1/1/2027 **[Foley]**; for the other
  states, fetch the date — it is not carried in this file.)
- Keep the HSR-era tag from `gather-prompts.md` as well. A row can carry both.

## Output template (Section A line, Section E.2a, E.2b, E.3, desk-read line)

**Section A, first line.** "The closing date here will be set by state health care regulators, not by U.S.
antitrust review. [HSR filing date]; [health care filings due date]." Classification: **dual-gated, state-led**
unless the evidence shows HSR leads. Where the state list cannot be verified at T+0, classify as *conditionally*
dual-gated and name the fact that decides it (this is the rule already in SKILL.md).

**E.2a Named-state build (perimeter test).** One row per state in scope:

| State | Regime | Notice or approval | Trigger test | Our data (source; proxy labelled) | Clock (from complete notice) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Status is one of: **Applies** / **Likely** / **Probable** / **Possible — verify** / **Notice only** / **Not a
transaction gate**. Add grouped rows for states with no transaction law (they fall under Layer B) and for the
licensure layer ("per-state tiers from the P1 and P6 pulls"). Rank states by regime and clock, not by site count.
Cite where each site count comes from and say it is a proxy.

**E.2b Earliest-close floors and tails.**

| Item | Filed [latest permitted date] | Filed [early date] | Versus base close |
|---|---|---|---|

Rows: each lead-time floor (30, 60, 90 days), each comprehensive-review tail, and any conditional row such as
Maine. Add one sentence saying whether the tails are timing or completion risks, with the days inside the outside
date.

**E.3 additions.** Precedent rows carry the regime-era tag and show days from announcement and from filing.

**Closing-condition status line.** For each state, one of: listed in the Disclosure Letter (cite) / not listed /
list unfiled. "List unfiled" is allowed only beside a derived perimeter and a watch list, never alone.

**Desk-read line.** "State health approvals set the date. Earliest close [date] on lead times. Tails run to
[date] ([states]). [Conditional state] could push to [date] if it applies."

## OPCH worked example (illustrative; as of 10/6/2026; re-verify before reuse)

Agreement dated 10/5/26; HSR filing due 11/3/26 (20 business days); health care filings due 11/18/26 (30 business
days); outside date 10/5/27; base close 4/30/27.

| Item | Filed 11/3/26 | Filed 11/18/26 | Versus 4/30/27 base |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor, Oregon / Illinois / New York (30 days) | 12/3/26 | 12/18/26 | well before |
| Floor, Massachusetts / Washington (60 days) | 1/2/27 | 1/17/27 | well before |
| Floor, California (90 days) | 2/1/27 | 2/16/27 | before |
| Oregon comprehensive review (180 days) | 5/2/27 | 5/17/27 | 17 days later |
| Massachusetts cost review (about 215 days) | 6/6/27 | 6/21/27 | 52 days later |
| Maine, if it applies and notice cannot be filed before 1/1/27 (180 days) | n/a | 6/30/27 | 61 days later; 97 days inside the outside date |

Perimeter notes: of the states a broker named by site count (CA, TX, WA, FL, LA, TN, VA, OH, IL, NY, OR, MA),
six have a Layer A law (CA, WA, IL, NY, OR, MA) and six do not (TX, FL, LA, TN, VA, OH), so those six sit in Layer
B only. OPCH filed an Oregon notice in 2023 (Amedisys), so Oregon is likely. Massachusetts turns on the parent
revenue test, which the acquirer's parent likely meets, so it is probable pending a Massachusetts nexus check.
Maine is possible and must be run down.
