# Cross-Deal Heuristics and Precedent Calibration

Patterns accumulated across prior proxy engagements. Use them to sharpen [Critical Analysis] paragraphs and Section 6 — cite the pattern (and precedent deal where apt) rather than asserting a standard from nowhere. Never force a pattern onto a deal it doesn't fit; the filing's own facts govern.

## Red-flag clusters (elevated appraisal scrutiny)

- **Single-bidder dynamics + holiday-period timeline + no special committee** is the recurring cluster. Any one alone warrants a flag; the cluster together warrants elevated appraisal scrutiny and a red-flag callout early in the report.
- **Management single-trigger equity acceleration** is a consistent conflict marker — always compare against rank-and-file double-trigger treatment. Divergence between cohorts is the finding.
- **Financial advisor contingent fee concentration** (high % of total fee tied to closing) is a standard independence flag — quantify the percentage and state it; don't just note "contingent fees exist."
- **280G top-up pool risks for specific officer cohorts** should be flagged explicitly in the golden parachute analysis — a top-up pool is the company paying the executives' excise tax to smooth the path to closing.
- **Management packages negotiated before price was final** — check the chronology in the background section against the "Interests of Directors and Officers" disclosure.

## Process-defect patterns (from the LPRO/ANV exemplar)

- **Buyer-as-commercial-partner chokehold**: when the acquirer (or its parent) is the target's key commercial counterparty with information rights and partnership leverage, it contaminates the entire price-discovery process — the market check is structurally deterred (rivals need the partner's cooperation), the premium narrative is self-serving, and topping-bid optionality is largely academic. This single fact can be the decisive structural finding of the whole report.
- **Forecast divergence**: when the filing discloses multiple projection sets, always compare them — which case underpins the fairness opinion vs. which case was shown to the buyer in marketing. A conservative case selected for the opinion while a ~3× richer case marketed the company is a core appraisal exhibit; quote the board's justification and state the petitioner's argument.
- **Financing-source concerns as negotiating cover**: a buyer citing its "financing sources'" worries to cut price — then funding largely with affiliate cash and no financing condition — used the financing narrative as a price lever, not a real constraint. Check the bid chronology against the sources table.
- **Support agreements that waive appraisal**: insiders pre-committing to tender AND waiving §262 rights shrink the dissenting pool, improve buyer certainty, and reveal negotiators' alignment with closing over price. Always check support agreements for appraisal waivers.
- **Outside-date mechanics as a signal**: an automatic extension triggered only by one unsatisfied condition (e.g., HSR-only) is the parties' own statement of their primary perceived timing risk.

## Appraisal calibration

- **Deal price positioning within (vs. above) the DCF range is the primary appraisal case calibration metric.** A deal price inside the advisor's own DCF range — especially in the lower half — is the core exhibit; a deal price above every methodology's range largely defeats the case.
- **Verify jurisdiction early — it can zero the thesis**: UK schemes of arrangement → no appraisal rights (Centessa/Eli Lilly). DGCL §262 → appraisal available, subject to the §262(b) market-out exceptions. Canadian/Chinese deals → check for a dissent cap giving the buyer a walk-away.
- Process quality feeds the appraisal case: a conflict-dense single-bidder deal (LPRO pattern) supports "fair value exceeds deal price" narratives; a genuine full auction undercuts them.

## Structure effects on the arb

- **Fixed-ratio all-stock merger of equals** (OLN/HUN pattern) → the arb thesis is a **convergence trade** with no premium or appraisal optionality. Don't manufacture an appraisal angle where the structure forecloses it.
- **CVRs require separate valuation treatment** and introduce binary risk into spread math (Centessa/Lilly pattern). State the CVR's milestones and payment terms verbatim; treat headline consideration and CVR value as distinct components in the thesis.
- **Subsidiary-merger structures can preserve target debt covenants** and defeat change-of-control put analysis (OLN/HUN) — check the structure before assuming existing-debt puts trigger.
- **All-cash tender offers** compress the timeline (20 business days + back-end merger) — process defects matter more because the market check window is shorter; appraisal attaches at the second-step merger.

## Discipline reminders

- A heuristic is a prior, not a fact — the report cites the filing; the heuristic only shapes where to dig and how hard.
- When a deal matches no pattern, say nothing rather than stretching an analogy. Novel structure is itself worth a [Critical Analysis] note.
