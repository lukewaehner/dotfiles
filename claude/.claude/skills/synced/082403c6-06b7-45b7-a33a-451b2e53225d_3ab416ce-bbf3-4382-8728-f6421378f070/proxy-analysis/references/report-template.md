# Proxy Analysis — Report Template

This template defines the house **format** — the exemplar for architecture, visual system, and callout voice is the 2026 Open Lending / ANV 14D-9 analysis. The exemplar sets the look and the scan architecture, **not a depth ceiling**: the research behind the report should meet or exceed it, and no finding is dropped to match its length. Follow the document architecture, table specs, and callout conventions below. Subsection *titles* adapt to the deal's own story; the *slots* do not.

## Document format (the .docx)

**Page architecture, in order:**

1. **Title block (page 1)** — no heading page break before Deal Snapshot:
   - Report label: "MERGER ARBITRAGE ANALYSIS"
   - Target line: "[Target legal name] (EXCHANGE: TICKER)"
   - Deal line: "Acquisition by [Acquirer] ([parenthetical identity]) — $X.XX per Share, [structure descriptor]"
   - Source line: "Source: [operative filing] filed [date] · Merger Agreement dated [date]"
   - **Deal Snapshot table** (label column bold, no header row): Target | Acquirer (entity + owners + merger sub) | Structure | Price | Equity value | Premium (per Co., all disclosed reference points) | Termination fee ($ and %) | Financing (amounts + "NO financing condition" if true) | Regulatory (regimes) | Minimum condition | Offer/vote period (commencement, expiration, Outside Date + extensions) | Appraisal rights (available/not, statute) | Financial advisor (+ opinion date). Adapt rows to the deal — a one-step merger swaps tender rows for vote/record-date rows.
   - Italic footnote: *"Prepared for internal investment use. Analytical commentary reflects a skeptical reading of management and board rationales where conflicts are present. Verbatim filing language is quoted in italics; bracketed text resolves ambiguity only."*
2. **Conclusions (page 2, page break after the title block)** — the report's verdicts before anything else: the Bottom-Line Arbitrage Thesis callout plus the Section Verdicts table. See spec below. Composed LAST, placed FIRST.
3. **Table of Contents (page 3)** — lists Conclusions and Sections 1, 1A, 2–6.
4. **Sections 1, 1A, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6** — page break before each Section heading.
5. **End note** (italic, small, final page): *"Prepared from the [Target] [filing] (filed [date]) and Annex [X] ([advisor] opinion dated [date]). All quotations are verbatim from that filing. This document is an internal analytical work product and is not legal, tax, or investment advice."*

**Visual system** (approximate hexes; keep consistent):

- Running header on every page: left "MERGER ARB · [TARGET] ([TICKER]) / [ACQUIRER]", right "Confidential — Internal", small gray caps, thin rule underneath. Footer: centered gray "Page N".
- Section headings: large bold navy (#1F3864) "Section N — Title" with a blue rule (#2E74B5) beneath. Subsections: bold medium blue (#2E74B5), numbered "N.N Title".
- **CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout**: light blue fill (#EAF1FA), thick dark-blue left bar (#2E74B5), small bold navy caps label "CRITICAL ANALYSIS" on its own line, then paragraphs.
- **RED FLAG callout**: light red fill (#FDECEA), thick dark-red left bar (#C00000), small bold red caps label "RED FLAG".
- Data tables: navy header row (#1F3864) with white bold text; alternating white/#F2F2F2 body rows; first column left-aligned, numeric columns right- or center-aligned (explicit alignment arrays); **bold the decisive row** and use red text (#C00000) only for the adverse decisive datum (e.g., "Offer BELOW midpoint (~$3.30)").
- Verbatim quotes italic; bracketed insertions only to resolve ambiguity.

**Callout conventions** — the analytical voice of the document:

- **RED FLAG** = a documented process/conflict deficiency (a fact pattern). **CRITICAL ANALYSIS** = analytical assessment (the skeptical read). Both are used inline throughout the report, adjacent to the facts they interpret — several per section is normal.
- Every callout opens with a **bold lead-in verdict sentence**, then one or two support lines — the verdict argues; the support supplies quote, date, and number. Style exemplars (LPRO, compressed register):
  - *RED FLAG*: "**Commercial coercion in the auction.** At the same June 5 meeting, AmTrust *'requested a discussion regarding the Company's long-term strategy, noting that AmTrust may revisit the ongoing business relationship…'* — the Company's key commercial partner tying continued partnership to deal alignment. Leverage no arm's-length buyer has."
  - *CRITICAL ANALYSIS*: "**Self-fulfilling auction design.** The board skipped a market test because the dominant commercial partner could block any rival — treating [Buyer]'s veto as a given, not a problem to neutralize. In appraisal terms, exactly the structural impediment that cuts the weight a court gives 'deal price.'"

**Citation convention**: the title-block Source line and end note establish that all quotes come from the operative filing. Inline citations are needed only when quoting a *different* document — e.g., "(OTP §9)" for the Offer to Purchase, "(Merger Agreement §6.2)". Give exact dates for every event and exact dollars for every figure.

**Depth vs. scannability**: skimmable is a formatting property, not a length property — **restructure, never abridge**. Research depth is invariant and length follows the deal's evidence (a conflict-dense deal runs longer). Optimize for the scanner: every prose subsection opens with a **bold one-line takeaway** stating the finding, with support beneath in short paragraphs; anything with three or more parallel facts goes in a table; callouts open with bold verdict lead-ins. A scanner reading only the bolded lines, tables, and callout lead-ins gets every finding — the underlying quotes and citations sit beneath for the reader who drills in. Density comes from quotes, dates, and tables — never prose padding.

**Presentation units — bullets and tables, not prose**: every subsection = bold one-line takeaway, then labeled bullets ("**Label** — fact, date, $, trimmed quote") and/or a table. Narrative subsections are dated bullet timelines, not story paragraphs. Quotes trimmed to the load-bearing fragment. Prose paragraphs are rare exceptions capped at two sentences. Callouts: bold verdict + one or two support lines carrying quote, date, number. Each argument made fully once, cross-referenced in a clause everywhere else. **Page test**: any rendered page that reads as a block of continuous text fails.

## Conclusions (upfront)

The first content page after the Deal Snapshot — a scanner who stops here has every verdict. Two elements, composed AFTER Section 6 is done (they are the distillation, not a preview):

**1. Bottom-Line Arbitrage Thesis** — one CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout, 3–5 bold-lead paragraphs. Standard movement:

1. **The decisive structural fact** — the single feature that contaminates or cleanses everything (e.g., "The cleanest structural short-seller's-mirror in this deal is the buyer's identity.").
2. **The price path** — what the bid chronology proves ("Price walked the wrong way.").
3. **The valuation positioning** — deal price vs. the waterfall's decisive method ("$3.15 sits below the DCF midpoint.").
4. **"Live angles:"** — the actionable enumeration: appraisal case strength, topping-bid optionality, spread character — and the asymmetry between them ("The asymmetry favors appraisal, not a bidding war.").

**2. Section Verdicts table** (required; navy header: Section | Verdict) — one row per Section 1 / 1A / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6, each verdict a single sentence with a bold lead stating that section's conclusion (e.g., Section 1: "**Single-bidder negotiation dressed as a limited market check** — price walked from a $3.50 written IOI to $3.15."). Cross-reference, don't re-argue: each row's language must match the section's own assessment, and the row points the scanner to where the support lives.

# Section 1 — Background of the Merger

Numbered subsections **1.1–1.8**. Titles 1.1–1.3 are narrative and deal-specific (e.g., "1.2 The AmTrust Round — and an Embedded Threat"); the analytical slots are fixed:

**1.1 Start of Process & Historical Interest.** When the advisor was engaged and why; whether the process was proactive or reactive to an unsolicited approach; historical expressions of interest predating the formal process. RED FLAG any timing coincidence with executive compensation changes or market events (new employment agreements bracketing the approach; comp amendments before signing).

**1.2–1.3 Process timeline** (one or more subsections as the story requires). Dated bullets, not narrative: one bullet per bid round, threat, pause, structure change, re-emergence — each carrying its date and the trimmed load-bearing quote. RED FLAG commercial coercion (a bidder that is also a key partner tying business relationship to deal alignment). CRITICAL ANALYSIS the board's process choices (declining a broad process, accepting a buyer's veto power as given, buyer-side delays the target paid for).

**1.4 Bid Chronology** (required table): Date | Party / Event | Price / Share | Context. One row per bid, revision, pause, and signing. Follow with a RED FLAG or CRITICAL ANALYSIS on what the path shows (e.g., accepted below the buyer's own written IOI after leverage evaporated).

**1.5 Scope of the Market Check.** How many parties, what mix (financial/strategic), when relative to the favored bidder's bids and signing deadline, outcomes (declined / NDA / bid). CRITICAL ANALYSIS: was it designed to create tension or to paper the record — count, timing, concurrency with price negotiation, structural deterrents.

**1.6 Confidentiality, Standstill & Exclusivity.** NDA counts and dates; standstill terms and fall-aways; "don't ask, don't waive" provisions (state "Not disclosed." if silent — and flag the gap); exclusivity grants, expirations, and any price-floor conditions.

**1.7 Post-Signing Market Check / Go-Shop.** Go-shop or no-shop, fiduciary-out mechanics, any post-signing bids. If there is no go-shop after a thin pre-signing check, that is a RED FLAG ("the price was never exposed to a clean, competitive market at any point").

**1.8 Auction Dynamics — Assessment.** Prose verdict on the process: full auction / limited market check / single-bidder negotiation, and whether it was structured to maximize value or to confirm a favored counterparty.

# Section 1A — Bidder Characteristics Table

Own section, one required table. Preamble line: "Built solely from the background section. Where the filing does not disclose a detail, the cell states 'Not disclosed.'"

Columns: **Bidder | Type | Size / Industry | Proposal Details | Outcome | Interloper Odds**. One row per distinct bidder; anonymous market-check parties grouped as one row when the filing groups them (e.g., "8 market-check parties"). Verbatim language only; quote the filing's own descriptors. Interloper odds reasoned strictly from background-section text (standstills, stated openness, board's own probability statements).

# Section 2 — Governance and Conflicts of Interest

**2.1 Board Committees.** Was an independent special committee formed? If oversight went to a pre-existing committee, name its members, formation date, and each member's economic stake. Quote the board's own disclaimers ("not as a result of any perceived or actual conflict") and test them: RED FLAG when the negotiating body is composed of the most conflicted insiders.

**2.2 Supporting Stockholders & Voting Power** (+ required insider table: Holder | Shares | Cash for Shares | Role / Affiliation). Tender/voting/support agreements — who, what %, and critically whether they **waived appraisal rights**. CRITICAL ANALYSIS: differing economics vs. minority holders (fund life, cost basis, liquidity needs; appraisal waivers shrinking the dissenting pool).

**2.3 Management Post-Closing Arrangements.** Buyer statements prioritizing management retention; disclosed employment discussions and their timing vs. price negotiation; the filing's denials, quoted and weighed against expressed buyer intent.

**2.4 Single- vs. Double-Trigger Asymmetry.** Equity treatment (options/RSUs/PSUs) at the effective time; single-trigger acceleration for executives vs. double-trigger cash severance and rank-and-file treatment; any single-trigger bonuses. CRITICAL ANALYSIS: what the structure pays management to do (close, regardless of price).

**2.5 Financial Advisor Independence** (+ required economic-ties table: item | From [Target] | From [Buyer & affiliates]) covering the disclosure period's fees each way, equity holdings, engagements for buyer affiliates, and this deal's fee split — fixed (opinion) vs. contingent (closing), with the contingent percentage computed. RED FLAG lopsided economics ("the advisor is paid ~50× more by the buyer's affiliate than by its own client") and fee contingency.

**2.6 Exculpation, Indemnification & D&O Tail.** Tail policies (years, premium caps), indemnification scope — quote when it expressly covers "the process conducted by the Board" in this very transaction.

---

# Section 3 — Regulatory Approvals

**3.1 Required Approvals & Status** (required table): Approval | Authority | Status / Timing | Notes. HSR and any of: CMA, EC, SAMR, Canada, ACCC, Bundeskartellamt, CFIUS, NRC, state insurance/PUC/AG, FDI regimes — only as the filing requires. Below the table: closing conditions tied to approvals, and **read the Outside Date mechanics as a signal** (an extension triggered only by one regime names the parties' own primary timing risk).

**3.2 Regulatory Risk — Assessment** (CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout). Substantive risk vs. structural friction; and specifically **challenge the board's regulatory-risk claims when used to favor the winning bidder or justify rejecting alternatives** — regulatory-speed arguments are a standard cover story.

---

# Section 4 — Financing of the Merger

**4.1 Sources** (required table): Source | Amount | Terms — equity commitments, debt commitments, target's own cash if used, total vs. required funds. State plainly whether there is a financing condition; quote specific-performance rights.

**4.2 Existing Debt.** Repaid, refinanced, or left outstanding; change-of-control provisions. A subsidiary-merger structure can preserve covenants — check before assuming puts trigger.

**4.3 Financing Risk — Assessment** (CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout). Real funding risk given commitments and sponsor cash; and check whether the *financing narrative was used as a price lever* during negotiation ("financing-source concerns" justifying cuts by a buyer that ultimately funds with affiliate cash).

---

# Section 5 — Miscellaneous Terms

**5.1 Tax Treatment.** U.S. holders; non-U.S. holders (state where the filing punts to another document); any structuring elements (or their absence, consistent with all-cash).

**5.2 Appraisal Rights.**
- Availability, with statute; quote the filing's own notice language and any reservation that the buyer/company may argue fair value is *less* than deal price; statutory interest mechanics.
- **Procedural prerequisites** as a bullet list: demand deadlines, do-not-tender/do-not-vote requirements, continuous holding, petition window and who bears it, de-minimis dismissal thresholds (share % / $ value).
- **Case for Appraisal** (CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout): the process-defect checklist assembled from Sections 1–2 and the valuation positioning from 6.1 — then a **"Counterweight."** paragraph stating the honest other side (methods the offer clears, premium size, deal-price deference risk).

**5.3 Dissent Cap.** Any appraisal/dissent-based walk right or cap (Canadian/Chinese-style); if none, say so and identify the only tender-volume gate.

---

# Section 6 — Critical Transaction Analysis

**6.1 Valuation Waterfall** (required table): Methodology | Low | High | Offer position — one row per fairness-opinion methodology (comps variants, precedents, premia, analyst targets, DCF), plus a bold **OFFER** row. Bold the DCF row; red text where the offer fails a method ("Offer BELOW midpoint (~$X.XX)"). CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout: which methods the offer clears and which it fails, and what the advisor's methodology choices did to the answer.

**6.2 Forecast Divergence** (required table when the filing discloses multiple projection sets): ($M) | years — revenue/EBITDA/FCF rows per case, red-bold the suppressed richer case. Analyze which case underpins the fairness opinion vs. which was shown to the buyer, quote the board's justification, and state the petitioner's argument.

**6.3 Golden Parachute Compensation** (required table): Named Executive Officer | Cash | Equity | Perq./Benefits | Total — every NEO including departed officers (— for zeros). Below the table: trigger characterization (which components single- vs. double-trigger) and 280G treatment (gross-up / best-net cutback / top-up pool — flag pools explicitly).

**6.4 Rejected / Higher Offers** (CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout). Any rejected higher bids with premium math and rationale credibility; where the only higher number was the buyer's own earlier indication, say exactly that and reframe the question ("why did the board's leverage to hold [Buyer] to $X.XX disappear").

**6.5 Governance Concerns — Summary Register** (required table): Concern | Why it matters. One row per defect, distilled from Sections 1–2 (conflicted buyer, no committee, captured advisor, suppressed forecast, price cut, trigger asymmetry, no go-shop…).

**6.6 Incentive Alignment** (RED FLAG callout). Whose incentives point where, in dollars — negotiators' payouts, sponsors' cash-outs and fund dynamics, advisor contingency — versus the minority holder's interest in price. Note when insiders waived the very remedy (appraisal) a price-maximizing holder would preserve.

**6.7 Completion Risk** (required table): Risk | Level | Assessment. Rows: Financing; Regulatory; Minimum tender / vote; Interloper / topping bid; Stockholder litigation. Levels: Low / Low–Med / Med / Elevated / High — with one-line justification each.

**6.8 Independent Director Perspectives.** Dissents, abstentions, separately voiced views — and when there are none, say what the absence means (unanimity from an unconflicted-committee-less board is a governance gap, not evidence on price).

**6.9 Bottom Line** (CRITICAL ANALYSIS callout, bold lead-in "Recommendation framing for the book."). The actionable verdict: how to play the spread, where the value tail lives (appraisal vs. topping bid), and why the asymmetry points the way it does. This is the same content as the upfront thesis — the upfront version is written from this one.
